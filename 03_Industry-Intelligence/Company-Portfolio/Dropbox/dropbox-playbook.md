## The Sharpest Framing: Dash as Visibility + Observability for Knowledge Work

Dropbox Dash is essentially visibility and observability applied to knowledge work — the same discipline already applied to IT operations, just scaled to a company's entire content layer.

**The IT parallel:**
- ServiceNow = visibility + observability for IT incidents
- PIPR = a custom observability layer surfacing signals before they escalate
- Dash = the same underlying philosophy, applied to all knowledge work across an entire company

**The genuine irony worth naming:** Dropbox built Dash because knowledge workers were drowning in too much content across too many tools — structurally the same problem IT teams face with too many alerts across too many systems. Same pain, different layer.

**The one-liner:** "Dash is essentially visibility and observability for knowledge work — the same discipline I apply to IT operations, just applied to the entire company's content layer."

**On DocSend specifically:** "Email with observability" is a clean, accurate plain-English description — DocSend is functionally analytics bolted onto email attachments.

## Apple → Dropbox CSM Bridge

**The Apple motion is post-sale CSM in practice:**
- A break-fix appointment = the customer already has the product, something isn't working, the job is restoring trust *and* solving the problem
- Under-1% looper rate = a retention metric — don't lose the customer twice
- Technical training for SMB owners = exactly the workshop/enablement motion a CSM performs
- The 3A framework = a relationship-repair playbook, directly transferable

**The talking point:** "At Apple, post-sale is where loyalty is built or lost. A customer who has a problem and gets an excellent resolution becomes more loyal than one who never had a problem in the first place. That's the CSM mindset — the relationship deepens through how adversity is handled, not just through smooth success."

## Personal Origin Story — Why Dropbox

"A Dropbox user since the early days — remember when it was just the little folder on the desktop that made file sync feel like magic. What's exciting now is the Dash direction — turning that trusted foundation into an AI layer connecting everything. That's a harder problem than storage, and a more interesting CSM challenge. Helping enterprise customers realize the value of AI-powered search requires real adoption work, not just onboarding."

**30-second pitch draft:** "14 years in enterprise IT — most recently as the identity and infrastructure lead at Alberta Health Services, serving 160,000 users across 900 facilities. The way to frame it: running an internal CSM function at enterprise scale — onboarding, adoption, health monitoring, executive relationships, voice of the customer back to the broader team. This is a deliberate move into external-facing CSM at a product company, and Dropbox stood out from years of using the product plus how genuinely exciting the Dash pivot is. AI-powered work is exactly where the next chapter of this career should be built."

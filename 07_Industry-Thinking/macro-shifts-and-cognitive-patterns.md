## The Expertise Trap: When Judgment Becomes a Filter

Deep expertise is an asset — until it starts filtering out real signals.

> "The better you get at something, the harder it can be to evaluate new ways of doing it."

The mechanism:

| Stage | What Happens |
|---|---|
| Early career | Open to everything — pattern matching actively building |
| Mid-career | Judgment develops — filters out noise effectively |
| Senior level | Same filter that blocks nonsense also blocks genuine shifts |

This isn't closed-mindedness. It's a structural side effect of earned expertise.

**At the company level:** Success creates processes and incentives that are hard to unwind.  
**At the individual level:** Success creates habits, instincts, and professional identity — equally hard to unwind.

Changing your mind stops feeling like learning. It starts feeling expensive — time, energy, ego, and the discomfort of rethinking a model that has served you.

### The Practical Adjustment

- **Stay curious for longer** before closing the loop on a new idea
- **Be skeptical, not dismissive** — there's a meaningful difference
- **Don't assume what made you right before will make you right again** — the conditions change

> "Sometimes the thing that made you good at your job is also the thing that makes a real shift harder to see."

## Infrastructure × Security Convergence

A structural shift in how enterprises organize security ownership:

**Old model:** Security teams accountable for risk, infrastructure teams own the systems.  
**Emerging model:** CISOs increasingly owning infrastructure — or at minimum, tightly co-owning it.

Why it's happening:

- Cloud, identity, and access are now inseparable from infrastructure
- Security decisions can't be made in isolation from infra decisions
- The attack surface lives inside the stack, not outside a perimeter

> "The future of security (and security products) sits at the intersection of infra and security — not in silos."

**Implication for practitioners:** IT/infra professionals with security depth are increasingly valuable. The gap between "I run the infra" and "I own the security posture" is closing — and those who operate across both sides are the ones getting hired.

## "Network Is Dead" — and Other Overconfident Takes

A recurring pattern in security discourse: sweeping declarations that replace nuance.

Examples of **overstatements that miss the point:**

| The Claim | What's Actually True |
|---|---|
| "Network is dead" | Network as a passive sensor is diminishing. Network as a control gate is more important than ever. |
| "AI will kill cybersecurity jobs" | AI handles code scanning well. Identity, cloud, network, and incident response still need humans. |
| "Just use MFA and you're safe" | MFA is one layer. OAuth tokens, machine identities, and supply chain access bypass it entirely. |

> "What's dead is network as a sensor. Network as a gate is more important than ever."

The pattern: as one control loses effectiveness, a different control gains importance. The field evolves; it doesn't disappear.

## Breach Response Framework (Contain, Preserve, Assess, Communicate)

From a security practitioner interview (Glenn Wilkinson, Random but Memorable podcast):

Even security-conscious people can be compromised. The framework for responding:

| Phase | Action |
|---|---|
| **Contain** | Limit the blast radius — isolate affected systems, revoke active sessions |
| **Preserve** | Don't destroy evidence — capture logs, snapshots, forensic artifacts |
| **Assess** | Understand what happened, what was accessed, what the scope is |
| **Communicate** | Notify affected parties — internal, legal, customers, regulators as required |

Relevant for incident response conversations in interviews and customer-facing CSE scenarios.

## Key Takeaways

- **Expertise filters signal AND noise** — the same judgment that protects you from hype can blind you to real shifts
- **Stay curious longer** — skepticism is healthy; dismissiveness is a trap
- **Infra and security are converging** — practitioners who span both sides are the ones in demand
- **Sweeping declarations ("X is dead") are almost always wrong** — look for what's shifting, not what's disappearing
- **Breach response has a framework** — Contain → Preserve → Assess → Communicate
